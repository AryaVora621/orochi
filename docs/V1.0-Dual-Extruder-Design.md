# Project Orochi - V1.0 Dual Extruder Design Plan

*Status: Design / Planning. Target MCU: BTT SKR 1.4 Turbo (single board). Tools: T0 and T1.*

This document is the engineering deep-dive for the **Version 1.0** stage defined in the
[main README](../README.md). It takes the two-tool Bowden concept and breaks it into a
buildable plan: mechanical layout, electronics and wiring, Klipper structure, calibration,
bill of materials, risks, and a definition of done. The goal of V1.0 is to prove the
CoreXY kinematics, the StealthChanger docking geometry, and the static-extruder Bowden
strategy on a single board before any slave MCU or custom PCB is introduced.

---

## 1. Overview and Goals

| Goal | How V1.0 achieves it |
| --- | --- |
| Prove CoreXY on the Ender 5 frame | Mercury One.1 conversion, MGN12 rails, single gantry |
| Two independent tools | StealthChanger shuttle + 2 docks, 2 V6 groove-mount hotends |
| Keep gantry mass minimal | Recycled Ender 3 extruders mounted statically, Bowden fed |
| Single-board simplicity | Everything (motion + 2 tools) runs on the SKR 1.4 Turbo |
| Clean tool swaps | Macro-driven park/pickup with standby retract and re-prime |

Success here de-risks V2.0, where the Creality 4.2.2 slave and RP2040 hub are added.

## 2. Mechanical Design

### 2.1 Frame and Motion (recap)

- Stock Ender 5 Pro cube retained as the structural body.
- Mercury One.1 CoreXY conversion on MGN12 linear rails (X and Y). The bed moves in Z, as on the stock machine.
- Two CoreXY motors are driven by the SKR `X` and `Y` stepper ports. Belt path and tensioning follow the Mercury One.1 manual.

### 2.2 Static Extruder Mounting

The defining V1.0 decision: the two extruder assemblies are **not** on the gantry. They are
bolted to the printer frame so the moving toolhead carries only a hotend, a plate, and a tube.

- **Hardware:** Full-size Ender 3 extruders (metal extruder body + 42-step motor) salvaged from retired machines.
- **Mount location:** Two mirrored printed brackets on the rear vertical extrusions of the cube, at roughly mid-height. Criteria:
  - Minimize Bowden length to each respective dock.
  - Keep tubes clear of the gantry travel envelope.
  - Provide vibration isolation so frame resonance does not couple into extrusion.
- **Separation:** The two extruders are mounted far enough apart to route tubes without crossing, reducing tangling during tool changes.

### 2.3 Tool Plates and Docks

- Two StealthChanger **tool backplates**, each carrying a lightweight V6-style **groove-mount hotend** (standard V6 or a bimetal-heartbreak variant). Groove mount is chosen for low mass and low cost.
- Two StealthChanger **Modular Docks** mounted at the front of the frame within shuttle reach.
- **Dock presence sensing:** one microswitch per dock, wired to spare SKR endstop ports. This feeds the klipper-toolchanger framework for tool-in-dock detection.

### 2.4 Bowden Umbilical

- PTFE tube (Capricorn XS recommended for lower friction) runs from each static extruder to its docked hotend.
- Tube lengths will **differ** between T0 and T1 because of the asymmetric mount. This is the primary reason per-tool pressure advance is required (see Calibration).
- Reverse-Bowden couplers at both ends reduce friction variance and wear at the extruder inlet.
- Tubes are clipped to the frame with printed PTFE guides so they do not whip during fast gantry moves.

## 3. Electronics and Wiring

### 3.1 SKR 1.4 Turbo Pin Map (V1.0)

All V1.0 functions live on the single SKR 1.4 Turbo. The TMC2209 drivers run in UART mode.

| Function | SKR port | Klipper section | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CoreXY X motor | X driver | `[stepper_x]` | TMC2209 UART |
| CoreXY Y motor | Y driver | `[stepper_y]` | TMC2209 UART |
| Bed Z motor | Z driver | `[stepper_z]` | TMC2209 UART |
| Extruder T0 | E0 (EXT0) driver | `[extruder]` in `T0.cfg` | TMC2209 UART |
| Extruder T1 | E1 (EXT1) driver | `[extruder]` in `T1.cfg` | TMC2209 UART |
| Hotend T0 heater | HE0 | heater in `T0.cfg` | PWM |
| Hotend T1 heater | HE1 | heater in `T1.cfg` | PWM |
| Hotend T0 thermistor | TH0 | sensor in `T0.cfg` | 4.7k pull-up |
| Hotend T1 thermistor | TH1 | sensor in `T1.cfg` | 4.7k pull-up |
| X homing | X-STOP | `[endstop_x]` | |
| Y homing | Y-STOP | `[endstop_y]` | |
| Dock T0 presence | Z-STOP (spare) | tool detection | microswitch |
| Dock T1 presence | E0-STOP or E1-STOP (spare) | tool detection | microswitch |

### 3.2 Power Budget

The Ender 5 Pro ships with a 24V PSU (commonly ~350W; **verify the label on your specific unit**).
Loads in V1.0:

- Bed heater: dominant load (verify wattage on your bed label).
- Two V6 hotends: ~40-60W each at temperature (heater cartridge dependent).
- Steppers, fans, Pi: minor relative to heaters.

**Planning rule:** Confirm PSU headroom with both hotends heating simultaneously plus a warm bed. If you fit high-power (50W+) cartridges and expect both tools hot at once, budget for a 24V 480-600W supply rather than the stock unit. Measure, do not assume.

### 3.3 Stepper and Driver Tuning

- TMC2209 UART on all axes and extruders.
- Extruders use `stealthChop` (silent, no sensorless homing needed). Set `run_current` per the salvaged motor's rating (typically 0.5-0.8A RMS for Ender 3 extruder motors; confirm the motor label).
- `hold_current` can drop after idle to reduce heat.
- Extruder motion uses `rotation_distance` (not legacy steps/mm) so e-steps are calibrated per physical extruder + tube path.

## 4. Klipper Configuration Layout

Proposed file structure (V1.0):

```
printer.cfg              # [mcu], [printer] corexy, steppers, heaters, toolchanger include
macros.cfg               # T0/T1, PARK_TOOL, PICKUP_TOOL, standby retract
T0.cfg                   # [extruder], heater, thermistor, gcode offsets for tool 0
T1.cfg                   # [extruder], heater, thermistor, gcode offsets for tool 1
toolchanger.cfg          # klipper-toolchanger [toolchanger] + [tool T0]/[tool T1]
```

Tool changing is handled by the **klipper-toolchanger** framework (the standard companion to
StealthChanger). Exact `Tx.cfg` syntax follows that project's documentation; the structure
below is illustrative of the V1.0 intent:

```ini
# T0.cfg (illustrative)
[extruder T0]
rotation_distance: 22.678        # calibrate per extruder + tube
pressure_advance: 0.55           # tune per Bowden length (T0)
pressure_advance_smooth_time: 0.03
heater_pin: PA1                  # SKR HE0
sensor_pin: PC0                  # SKR TH0

[tool T0]
gcode_x_offset: 0.0
gcode_y_offset: 0.0
# z offset calibrated per tool, applied at pickup
```

## 5. Calibration Plan

Performed in order, each verified before moving on:

1. **Motion:** home CoreXY, tune input shaper, verify squareness and belt tension.
2. **E-steps per extruder:** measure 100mm cold feed for E0 and E1 independently; set `rotation_distance` each.
3. **Per-tool pressure advance:** run a PA calibration pattern on T0 and T1 separately. Because the Bowden lengths differ, expect different values.
4. **Dock positions:** set dock approach/depart coordinates manually, then refine with the toolchanger auto-calibration.
5. **Per-tool Z offset:** with the tool mounted, probe and record the nozzle height difference between T0 and T1.
6. **PID tune:** PID autotune each hotend independently.
7. **Hotend temperatures:** verify both hold target within +/-2C under load.

## 6. Tool Change Sequence (V1.0)

Conceptual flow used by the macros:

```
Active tool printing -> print completes or slicer issues Tn
  -> STANDBY_RETRACT on current tool (avoid dock ooze)
  -> drop current tool to standby temp
  -> PARK_TOOL: move shuttle to current dock, release magnetic coupling
  -> move gantry to target dock
  -> PICKUP_TOOL: engage shuttle, confirm dock microswitch cleared
  -> apply target tool gcode/z offsets
  -> PRIME: re-heat to target, short purge + wipe
  -> resume printing with new active tool
```

Crash detection wraps the sequence so a missed dock triggers a controlled abort instead of a
gantry strike.

## 8. Risks and Open Questions

- **Bowden friction variance.** Different tube lengths and routing can cause inconsistent extrusion between tools. Mitigation: Capricorn tube, consistent reverse-Bowden couplers, per-tool e-steps and PA.
- **Dock ooze.** Parked hotends left loaded and hot blob the dock. Mitigation: standby retract + standby temperature + purge-on-pickup.
- **Pickup reliability.** Magnetic coupling must engage cleanly at speed. Mitigation: dock tuning, verified magnet strength, slow constrained approach.
- **Per-tool Z drift.** Nozzle heights differ and shift with wear. Mitigation: periodic probe offset recalibration.
- **PSU headroom.** Two simultaneous hotends plus bed may exceed the stock supply. Mitigation: measure and upgrade if needed.
- **Open question:** single Bowden vs. reverse-Bowden at the extruder inlet, to be decided during the first bench test.

---

## 9. Acceptance Criteria (Definition of Done for V1.0)

- [ ] CoreXY homes and prints a single-tool benchy on T0 with good quality.
- [ ] T0 to T1 swap completes with no manual intervention across 10 consecutive cycles.
- [ ] Both hotends hold temperature within +/-2C under load.
- [ ] No ooze blobs remain in either dock after a multi-tool print.
- [ ] Per-tool pressure advance tuned; no under-extrusion or corner rounding attributable to Bowden lag.
- [ ] Per-tool Z offsets recorded and reproducible.

When these are met, V1.0 is declared complete and V2.0 (slave board + RP2040 hub) begins.
